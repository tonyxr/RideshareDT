Revision 7: bounded demand repair, 2026-10-18

Only the rider-choice price-response path and its calibration contracts change.
PPOAgent.py, platform_mdp.py, driver_supply.py, pricing_models.py and the reward
objective are byte-identical to the revision-6 source. Revision 6 is untouched.
No existing policy is silently evaluated in a different demand environment.

Joint contextual heterogeneous logit
  anchor_i = existing contextual choice probability at the exogenous city-anchor
             quote for this trip (not either firm's chosen posted quote)
  h = city calibration median PriceThreshold / this rider's PriceThreshold
  U_i = log(anchor_i) - beta * h * shock_sensitivity
                        * (posted_fare_i - reference_trip_fare) / typical_city_fare
  U_outside = log(anchor_outside)
  P_i = softmax(U)_i across Firm1, Firm2 and NoRide

This removes the posted-price min gate, threshold salience multiplier,
fractional power of the gap, and context temperature from the marginal price
response. Existing context, loyalty, waiting and reputation at the anchor
remain explicit priors. When the first firm cannot serve, the available firm's
utility is compared with the same outside utility, without resurrecting the
old single-price acceptance gate. There is no tariff controller or policy
fallback. Cached thresholds in this path are validated, not clipped.

For a fixed rider opportunity at parity, the own-price elasticity is
  -beta * h * (reference_trip_fare/typical_city_fare) * (1 - P_own).
For a population it is weighted by P_own. The calibration computes beta
analytically from this aggregate moment. No reward, supply outcome, tariff
optimum, or RL policy enters that calculation.

User-approved transferred-prior sensitivity arms: -0.9, -1.15, -1.4, all retained.
Source: List, Muir, Pope and Sun (2023), Left-Digit Bias at Lyft, pp. 1-2:
https://s3.amazonaws.com/fieldexperiments-papers2/papers/00771.pdf
These are NOT city-specific estimates or confidence-interval endpoints. The
midpoint is an illustrative central prior, not a fitted empirical estimate.

Not identified/validated by this repair:
  * Baseline participation, actual cross-platform diversion, or app-entry demand.
  * The inverse-threshold heterogeneity assumption or conditional logit IIA.
  * Shock elasticities or long-run supply equilibrium.
  * Profit dominance or the economic value of dynamic pricing.
Fitting an assumed moment successfully is not external validation. Held-out
synthetic profiles test transfer within the simulator, not validity in a city.
If the resulting demand changes supply or the best constant, capacity and warm
starts must be remeasured. Old supply points are explicitly rejected because
they were fitted under a different demand contract.

New entrypoint: scripts/audit_joint_demand.py (no training or supply calibration).
Use --demand_calibration_artifact with one of its saved city/arm JSON files to
explicitly install the new model. Policy artifacts embed the exact parameters;
reload and city operating-point reuse verify equality. No artifact means the
legacy model for historical reproducibility, not an automatic runtime fallback.

Testing: tests/test_joint_demand.py (equations and properties),
tests/test_joint_demand_integration.py (actual city/cache/simulator/save-load paths),
plus the existing regression and flags-off identity tests. Existing tests still
protect retained behavior and are not removed just because their names refer
to an earlier revision. No production retraining is authorized by this audit.
